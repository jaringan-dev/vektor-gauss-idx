What is this tool? Vektor Gauss reads publicly available Indonesian market data (prices, foreign flow, per-broker trades, KSEI ownership) and flags stocks whose transaction pattern looks unusual. Every flag is produced by a fixed, written rule, so nothing here is a black box and nothing is a prediction. The page called Track record measures whether those flags have actually been useful. As of early November 2026 none had proven itself after trading costs; that page always shows the current verdict.

How to read a stock row

Take a row like this one (an example from a past session) on Foreign-flow screener: BBCA · Accumulation · 4,840 · −2.8% · foreign today −24.4B · foreign 1 week +486.6B.

  1. Price and change: the stock closed at Rp4,840, down 2.8% on the day.
  2. Foreign today vs 1 week: foreigners sold a net Rp24.4 billion today, but over five days they bought a net Rp486.6 billion. One quiet or negative day does not cancel a week of buying.
  3. Quadrant: "Accumulation" is the label for price down or flat while foreigners buy on balance over the week. It is a description, not a forecast.
  4. Why it appears: the patterns that fired. Click a stock for the full picture, or a pattern for its exact rule.
  5. Verdict on the pattern: shown on the stock page and on Track record. A pattern that has not passed is not a reason to act on its own.

What this tool cannot do

  • It is not investment advice, it does not predict prices, and it never tells you to buy or sell.
  • The free data has gaps: per-broker data covers only about 200 liquid stocks, and KSEI ownership is monthly and late.
  • Round-trip trading costs are assumed to be about {{ cost_bp }} bp ({{ '%.1f' % (cost_bp / 100) }}%). A pattern must still beat the average stock after that; as of early November 2026 none had.
  • The stop-loss and target levels on a stock page are mechanical arithmetic from volatility. They are not forecasts and the price can gap past them.
